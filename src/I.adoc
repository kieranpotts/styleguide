icon, iconic:: In danger of losing all meaning through overuse. Avoid unless something is genuinely widely regarded as iconic. See also <<overused-words,Overused words>>.

ie:: No full points; also "eg," not "e.g." See also <<abbreviations-and-acronyms,Abbreviations and acronyms>>.

in-depth:: "In-depth analysis," but "was looked at in depth."

index:: Plural "indexes," except in scientific and economic contexts: "indices."

indie:: For music, films, and so on; "Indy" is short for a publication named "the Independent."

infer, imply:: "To infer" is to deduce something from evidence; "to imply" is to hint at something. See also <<commonly-confused-words,Commonly confused words>>.

inflammable:: Means the same as "flammable"; the negative is "non-flammable."

inshallah:: Means "God willing" in Arabic.

internet, net, website, web, world wide web:: All lowercase.

into:: See also "onto."

invalid:: Means not valid, or of no worth; do not use to refer to disabled or ill people — see "disabled people, disabled persons."

invariable, invariably:: Means unchanging; often wrongly used to mean "hardly ever changing." See also <<commonly-confused-words,Commonly confused words>>.

Ireland, Irish Republic:: Not "Eire."

ironically:: Avoid when "strangely," "coincidentally," "paradoxically," or "amusingly" is meant. There are times when "ironically" is right, but it is too often misused.

-ise:: Not "-ize" at the end of a word, eg "maximise," "synthesise" (exception: "capsize"). This is a British-spelling convention; this standard's own default is American spelling — see the section introduction.

Islam:: The holy book of Islam is the Qur'an, not "Koran."

Islamic festivals:: "Ashura" — a day of voluntary fasting for Muslims; Shia Muslims also commemorate the martyrdom of Hussein, a grandson of the prophet, making it a day of mourning for their community. "Eid al-Adha" (Festival of Sacrifice) — celebrates the end of the hajj; "eid" already means "festival," so "Eid festival" is redundant. "Eid al-Fitr" — celebrates the end of Ramadan ("al-fitr" means the breaking of the fast); often called "Eid" in speech, but write the full name, and avoid "Eid al-Fitr festival" for the same reason. "eid mubarak" — not a festival but a greeting ("mubarak" means "may it be blessed"). "Lailat al-Qadr" and "Lailat al-Miraj" — Islamic holy days for study and prayer. "Milad al-Nabi" — celebrates the birth of the prophet; many Muslims disapprove of celebrating it. "Ramadan" — the month of fasting.

Islamist:: An advocate or supporter of Islamic fundamentalism.

it is:: Truncate to "it's" where natural, but do not overdo it.
